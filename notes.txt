In March 2024, the city of Oakridge launched the Aurora Renewable Energy Project, a joint municipal and private initiative designed to transition public infrastructure entirely to clean power by 2030. 
The project broke ground with the construction of a 150-megawatt solar farm on 450 acres of reclaimed industrial land in the eastern district. 
According to Dr. Elena Vance, the lead environmental engineer on the site, the solar array utilizes bifacial panels that capture sunlight from both sides, increasing energy yield by roughly 18% compared to traditional installations.During the first phase, which cost approximately $120 million, local contractors installed smart grid monitors across 14 public schools and municipal buildings. 
These monitors allow facility managers to track real-time electricity consumption and automatically route excess solar power back to the city's central battery storage facility. 
In its first six months of operation, the system reduced Oakridge’s municipal carbon emissions by 34% and saved the local government an estimated $1.2 million in utility expenses.
However, the project has faced minor hurdles. Unusually heavy spring rainfall delayed the installation of the central battery bank by six weeks, and local wildlife conservation groups raised concerns about fencing interfering with local deer migration paths. 
In response, project organizers announced in September 2024 that they would redesign 2 miles of perimeter fencing to include elevated wildlife crossing gates before Phase Two begins in early 2025.
